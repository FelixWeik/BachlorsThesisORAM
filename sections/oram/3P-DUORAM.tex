\label{sec:3pDuoram}
\textit{DUORAM} is an ORAM-implementation by Vadapalli et al. \cite{vadapalli2023duoram}.
It serves as a 2-party or 3-party protocol.
This research will focus solely on the 3-party variant.
DUORAM uses (2,2)-DPFs (\ref{sub:DistributedPointFunctions}) to represent PIR and PIR-writing queries (\ref{sub:PrivateInformationRetreival}).
All of the following protocols and notations are as described by Vadapalli et al. \cite{vadapalli2023duoram}.

At a high level, the database $D$ is additively secret shared among two non-colluding parties $P_0,P_1$.
Both parties hold the additive shares of the target index they want to access in form of the shifting vector $e_i$.
Writing accesses are done via additively sharing the value both parties want to write.
This value is denoted with $M$.
These values are stored in so called \textit{RDPF-Triples}.
RDPF means DPF for accessing and manipulating the database.
Evaluating them gives six pairs of word vectors with the property that $t_0^1+t_1^1=t_0^2+t_1^2=t_0^3+t_1^3=e_i$ and $v_0^1+v_1^1=v_0^2+v_1^2=v_0^3+v_1^3=M\cdot e_i$.

Also, each party carries a \textit{Blinding Factor ($\zeta$)} that will be used to mask its share of the database $D_i$.
The blinded share will be denoted with $\widetilde{D}_i\leftarrow D_i+\zeta_i$.
Additionally, each party carries the blinded database of the other party.
This means party $P_b, b\in\{0,1\}$ holds $\widetilde{D}_{1-b}$.

This 3-party variant also has a stateful auxiliary part $P_2$.
It does neither hold the database nor index shares and merely facilitates the computation.

\subsection{Reading and Writing}
\label{sub:DuoramReadingAndWriting}
\paragraph{Reading:}
\label{par:DuoramReading}
The client wants to read at index $i^*$ from $D$.
This notation is equivalent to $D[i^*]=\langle D,e_{i^*}\rangle$.
The algorithm works as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $P_2$ selects $p\in\{0,1\}^w$ uniformly at random and sends $y_1\leftarrow-\langle \zeta_0,t_1^3\rangle + p$ to $P_0$ and $y_1\leftarrow\langle\zeta_1,t_0^2\rangle -p$ to $P_1$.
    \item $P_0$ outputs $\text{read}_0\leftarrow\langle D_0+\widetilde{D}_1, t_0^1\rangle - \langle\zeta_0,t_0^3-t_0^1\rangle + y_0$.
    \item $P_1$ outputs $\text{read}_1\leftarrow\langle D_1+\widetilde{D}_0,t_1^1\rangle - \langle\zeta_1,t_1^2-t_1^1\rangle +y_1$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Writing:}
\label{par:DuoramWriting}
Both parties want to obtain shares of a new Database $D'$, whose $i^{*^{th}}$ value is updated by $M$.
Recall the word-vectors $v_b^i$ with $v_0^i+v_1^i=e_{i^*}\cdot M$.
This implies, one simply locally sets $D'_b\leftarrow D_b+v_b^1$ for $b\in\{0,1\}$.

\paragraph{Refresh Blinds:}
\label{par:RefreshBlinds}
The blinded, exchanged shares become "stale" after a single update operation.
This means $\widetilde{D}'_b\neq D_{b-1}+\zeta_{b-1}, \text{ for } b\in\{0,1\}$ after executing a write operation.
One needs to refresh them to guarantee correctness in the following operations.
This means party $P_b, b\in\{0,1\}$ has to refresh $\widetilde{D}_b$ so it matches $D_{1-b}+\zeta_{1-b}$ and party $P_{1-b}$ has to refresh its blind $\zeta_{1-b}$ before.

This specific operation will is not of great importance in this research.
If not specified differently, $\text{RefreshBlinds}$ will just be adapted to the given datasize.
The benevolent reader may read the more detailed research by Vadapalli et al. \cite{vadapalli2023duoram}.

\subsection{Preprocessing- and Online-Phase}
\label{sub:DuoramPreAndOnlinePhase}
DUORAM works in two phases, namely the \textit{Preprocessing Phase} and the \textit{Online Phase}.
The intention is to shift the calculation and communication expensive part to the preprocessing phase to have everything ready for reading and writing.

\subsubsection*{Preprocessing-Phase}
\label{subsub:DuoramPreprocessing}
Main goal of this phase is to generate the DPF-Triples (\ref{sub:DistributedPointFunctions}) on the same, random target index.
DUORAM uses a (2+1)-Party protocol for generating their DPFs, which allows to replace the trusted source by a stateless third party $P_2$.
First, both parties $P_0,P_1$ choose a random index $\widetilde{ri}_0,\widetilde{ri}_1$ with $\widetilde{ri}_0\oplus\widetilde{ri}_1=ri$.
The DPFs will be generated on these target indices using the BGI-construction (\ref{sub:BGIDPFConstruction}).
The result is the DPF-triples $(\overline{k}_0^1,\overline{k}_0^2,\overline{k}_0^3)$ and $(\overline{k}_1^1,\overline{k}_1^2,\overline{k}_1^3)$.
$P_0$ will then send $\overline{k}_0^2$ to $P_2$ and $P_1$ will send $\overline{k}_1^3$ to $P_2$.
Note the usage of XOR shares here while the rest of DUORAM works on additive shares.
Therefore, using a MPC share conversion procedure, the XOR shares are turned to additive shares $ri_0,ri_1$ with $ri_0+ri_1=ri$.
This will also be done to change the evaluated vectors $v_b^i,t_b^i$ representing the leafs and flag bits to additive shares.
A detailed explanation of the MPC protocol to change from XOR to additive shares can be found in the research of Vadapalli et al. \cite{vadapalli2023duoram}.

The Preprocessing-Phase results in both parties $P_0,P_1$ holding DPF-Triples and their evaluations in form of additive shares. 
$P_2$ holds the DPFs $(\overline{k}_0^2, \overline{k}_1^3)$.

\subsubsection*{Online-Phase}
\label{subsub:DuoramOnlinePhase}
This phase includes read and write operations executed by $P_0,P_1$.
Both parties hold the DPF-Triples on random target-indices $ri_0,ri_1$.
To write or read from the non-random target index $i^*$ both parties need to execute the \textit{Cyclic-Shift Protocol} (\ref{sub:CyclicShiftingProtocol}).
DUORAM uses cyclic shifting in two flavors to postpone decision that have not been made in the \textit{Preprocessing-Phase}:
Namely postponing the decision of where to read/write or to postpone the decision of what to write.

\paragraph{Postponing the decision of \textit{where} to read or write:}
This protocol adjusts the shares of the shifting-vector $e_{ri}$ to the target index $e_{i^*}$.
Thus, the target index changes from the random $ri$ to the actual target index $i^*=i^*_0+i^*_1$.
Now, both parties need to get vectors $v_0+v_1=e_{i^*}$ to represent the shifting vector.

They exchange $i^*_b-ri_b$, reconstruct $cs=(i_0^*+i_1^*-ri_0-ri_1)$ and cyclic right shift $v_b$ by $cs$.
$P_2$ receives $i^*_b-ri_b$ from $P_b$ and can also compute $cs$.

\paragraph{Postponing the decision of \textit{what} to write:}
Recall the goal of the writing-operation: Adding $M=M_0+M_1$ at the already shifted target index $i^*$ to the database $D$.
$P_0,P_1$ exchange $M_b+F_b$ (with $F$ being the final correction word).
This allows both parties to reconstruct $M-(v_0[i^*]+v_1[i^*])$ which will serve as the new final correction word $F'$ to write $M$ in the desired location.

After performing the cyclic shifts, the DPFs have to be evaluated to receive the new vectors $\tilde{t}_b^i,\tilde{v}_b^i, (i\in\{1,2,3\},b\in\{0,1\})$ as described in \ref{sec:3pDuoram}.
Finally, all three parties execute the write or read protocol from \ref{sub:DuoramReadingAndWriting}.
